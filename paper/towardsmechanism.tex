\subsection{An interventional analysis}
\label{sec:swap}

The LRE of Eq.~\ref{eq:beta} is the sum of an \emph{anchor} $b_\beta$,
which does not depend on the subject, and a \emph{subject term}
$\beta W s$. The subject term has the form of NEL
(Eq.~\ref{eq:nel}).

% doesn't mean that tis form is differnet
%, except that its Jacobians are taken under the
%relation's own prompt.
We ask what each term carries by recombining
them. If the anchor carries the relation, pairing it with a
\emph{foreign} subject term from another relation should still
produce this relation's object; and if NEL captures the subject, it
should be able to stand in for the subject term.

We use two relation pairs that share their subjects:
\textsc{country\_capital\_city} and \textsc{country\_largest\_city} over
22 countries whose capital is not their largest city and for which the
model answers both relations correctly, and
\textsc{person\_plays\_position\_in\_sport} and
\textsc{person\_plays\_pro\_sport} over 24 athletes. Each subject is read
with the others as references, at $\beta = 3$.
Appendix~\ref{sec:swapdetail} describes the experiments in full.

\paragraph{The anchor carries the relation.} A foreign subject term
leaves the answer largely intact (Table~\ref{tab:swap}): with the
largest-city subject term, the capital anchor still names 12 capitals, as
many as with its own; each person anchor keeps 21 of 24 subjects with
the other relation's subject term. The anchor alone names no country's
capital or largest city, so these hits are composed rather than read off
the anchor. Nor do they depend on the particular references: over 500
anchors estimated from references disjoint from the test subjects, the
foreign subject term stays on average within one to two subjects of the
operator's own on all four relations.

\paragraph{The lens stands in for the subject term, but only
moderately.} NEL, computed under the neutral prompt with no relation
anywhere, recovers the operator for sport (21 against 22) and largely for
position (18 against 19), but for only 7 of the 12 capitals and 4 of the
12 largest cities that the operator itself gets right. What it supplies
is subject-specific: another subject's NEL falls to or below the
anchor alone. It is also less stable than the foreign subject
term. Over the same 500 anchors it ranges from 7 to 20 of 24 for
position, and for the country pair neither the anchor's references nor
NEL's own references dominate its variance.

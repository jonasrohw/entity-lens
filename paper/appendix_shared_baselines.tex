\section{Shared-split baseline comparisons}
\label{sec:shared-baselines}

We compare five readouts across seven models, then examine corpus-frequency
baselines for OLMo 3 7B. These first two tables report Spearman correlations with LRE
faithfulness across 25 relations, giving each relation equal weight.
The measurements use the current shared split and supersede the historical
unprefixed-prompt comparisons.

\paragraph{Matched protocol.}
After filtering objects whose first answer token does not identify a unique
complete object, seed 0 selects eight demonstrations, eight references, and
up to 100 test rows. All readouts receive the same test pool within a model;
tokenizer-dependent filtering can change membership across models.
The neutral prompt is exactly \texttt{\textbackslash n\{subject\} is known for}.
Models use pinned revisions, float32, eager attention, and one fixed layer
per model. The relation prompts, model answer tokens, and selected LRE
settings are held fixed.

\paragraph{Readout definitions.}
For subject state $s_e$, reference states $s_i$, and reference Jacobians
$J_i$, the differential and raw Jacobian readouts decode
$\operatorname{mean}_i J_i(s_e-s_i)$ and
$\operatorname{mean}_i J_i s_e$, respectively. Logit lens decodes $s_e$;
differential logit lens decodes $s_e-\operatorname{mean}_i s_i$.
Same-subject references are excluded, and final normalization is applied
once before the vocabulary head. Each relation's score is the negative
median $\log_{10}(\mathrm{rank}+1)$ of the model's answer token.

\paragraph{Attribute rate.}
Following \citet{geva2023dissecting} and the reconstruction of
\citet{gerstner2026weakening}, we measure overlap between filtered top-50
logit-lens predictions and subject attributes retrieved from Wikipedia.
The index uses the union of current test subjects; the source stopword,
length, and retrieval filters are retained. Relation scores average the
percentage overlap. Undefined rates are handled as noted in the table;
matched-row sensitivity results remain in the accompanying data.

Raw Jacobian has a larger point estimate than differential Jacobian in five
models. All seven paired 95\% relation-bootstrap intervals for their
difference include zero; these results do not establish a general advantage
for either readout. LRE hyperparameter selection remains in sample.

\subsection{OLMo corpus-frequency baselines}

Table~\ref{tab:shared-corpus-spearman} uses the completed scan of OLMo's

\paragraph{Counting and aggregation.}
A document contributes at most one mention per subject/object string.
Matching uses normalized literal names with Unicode word boundaries,
without alias resolution or a proximity constraint. With subject, object,
and joint counts $c_s$, $c_o$, and $c_{so}$, the conditionals are
$P(o\mid s)=c_{so}/c_s$ and $P(s\mid o)=c_{so}/c_o$.
Macro features average row-level quantities where both marginals are
observed. Micro conditionals divide summed joint counts by summed
marginals. The log of the mean joint count has the same Spearman correlation
as the mean joint count because the transformation is monotonic.

\paragraph{Interpretation and reproducibility.}
These are exploratory associations. Relations can share subjects and
objects, and the reported $p$-values are unadjusted. Conditional direction
also reflects marginal frequency: when $c_{so}>0$,
$P(o\mid s)/P(s\mid o)=c_o/c_s$.
The full measurements, Pearson results, coverage sensitivities, paired
intervals, and source hashes remain in
\texttt{code/results/shared\_baselines/}. The tables are generated
directly from the verified correlation records.

\subsection{Neutral-prompt variations}
\label{sec:prompt-variations}

Table~\ref{tab:shared-prompt-variations} collects the prompt experiments for
the differential Jacobian lens. For Qwen3 8B Base, we compare the original
prompt with six alternatives and the subsequently measured newline control.
The checkpoint, layer 19, reference and test rows, relation-prompt answer
tokens, and LRE outcomes are fixed within each protocol. The legacy split
contains 28 relations; the shared split contains 25. The lower panel reports
the original-to-newline comparison for all seven models on the shared split,
using each model's fixed layer.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
Qwen's shared-split correlation rises from $0.268$ under the original prompt
to $0.729$ with a leading newline; \texttt{Facts about \{s\}:} gives $0.788$.
These are exploratory sensitivity results, not held-out prompt selection.
Prefix wording can change both context and subject position. The newline
preserves Qwen's original content token IDs, while Mistral also retokenizes
the initial content. The legacy and shared protocols are not independent
replications, and no layer or LRE hyperparameter is retuned between prompts.

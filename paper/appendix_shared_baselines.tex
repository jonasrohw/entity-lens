\section{Full-evaluation protocol and corrections}
\label{sec:full-protocol}
\label{sec:numeric-objects}

All benchmark tables and figures in this paper use the original 28
relations, comprising 9,747 rows per model before the stated year-scoring
exception. Original strings, labels, row order within the recorded
selections, and duplicates are preserved. There is no correctness filter,
identifying-first-token filter, row cap or minimum-test-size cohort.

\paragraph{Demonstrations, references and evaluation.}
Each method uses eight demonstration rows and eight reference rows.
The LRE selection reproduces the original sequential shuffles with
seeds 0 and 1000; the lens selection uses seed 17. Every original row
is then evaluated, including both selections. For the lens, reference
comparisons sharing the query's subject string are omitted from its
average; the query is still scored, and duplicates remain in aggregate
statistics. LRE retains its original reference estimator. The different
demonstration contexts can produce different target tokens, which is
recorded rather than silently treated as a matched-context evaluation.

\paragraph{LRE selection.}
The primary outcome chooses a layer and scaling from the original
six-by-six grid on the full evaluation. Table~\ref{tab:full-sensitivity}
also reports the original fixed layer/scaling evaluated on every row.
Neither version is an independent held-out estimate. Full-data LRE
measurements and target tokens are reused for neutral-prompt changes;
only affected lens readouts are recomputed.

\paragraph{Gender wording.}
For a target in the female wording set, the accepted single-token,
leading-space terms are \texttt{woman, her, herself, she, hers, female,
girl}; for the male set they are \texttt{man, him, himself, he, his,
male, boy}. A lens rank is the best rank in the matching set; other
model targets remain unchanged. This rule is applied to every
vocabulary-rank readout in the baseline comparison. Dataset labels are
not rewritten. LRE faithfulness still requires its exact top-1 token to
match the model's top-1; the alternative wordings modify the lens axis.

\paragraph{Year tokenization.}
The answer boundary is checked with each actual tokenizer and the
joint prompt--answer string. Where necessary, a trailing space is
consumed in the relation prompt so that the next token is content.
A standalone first answer token \texttt{1} is distinguished from
multi-digit tokens such as \texttt{178}. Under the relation-level rule
used for the primary comparison, \textsc{presidents\_birth\_year} and
\textsc{presidents\_election\_year} are excluded for Mistral, Gemma and
both Qwen models, leaving 26 scored relations and 9,709 rows each.
The artifact occurs on all 19 birth-year rows and 16 of the 19
election-year rows in each affected model. The relation-level rule removes
both complete year relations, including the other three election-year
rows, from the primary correlation. The rule does not depend on measured
faithfulness. OLMo, GPT-J and
Pythia retain both year relations and all 9,747 rows. All original rows
and measurements remain available; the all-28 column in
Table~\ref{tab:full-sensitivity} shows the year-inclusive sensitivity.

\paragraph{Continent references.}
We retain the original seeded references for
\textsc{landmark\_on\_continent}; there is no diversity correction in
these reported values. Their selection and category counts are stored
with each measurement. This setting is applied consistently to both
methods and all models.

\paragraph{Leading newline and high norms.}
GPT-J 6B, Pythia 6.9B, Qwen3 8B Base and Qwen3.8 27B use
\texttt{\textbackslash n\{\} is known for}. A dagger marks these models
in the result tables. OLMo, Mistral and Gemma use
\texttt{\{\} is known for}. The newline addresses unusually large
subject-state norms when content occupies the first token position.
Table~\ref{tab:full-newline} reports the additional full-row GPT-J and
Pythia ablations. Qwen's previously adopted correction is retained.
The ablations change neither the LRE outcome nor its target tokens.

\paragraph{Numerical checks and provenance.}
Measurements use pinned checkpoint revisions, \texttt{float32}, eager
attention, and the architecture's actual final normalization and output
head. Scalar and batched Jacobian--vector products, finite differences,
and cached computations are numerically checked. The report validates
complete row multisets, duplicate counts, target IDs, reference counts,
and source hashes, and recomputes aggregates from per-row ranks.
Current numbers are generated from these records rather than copied from
historical tables. Earlier shared-split, capped, correctness-filtered,
and separate small-cohort numerical analyses are archived outside the
compiled paper and are not used to substantiate its current claims.

\paragraph{Readout definitions.}
The contrastive/differential Jacobian lens (CEL) decodes
$\operatorname{mean}_i J_i(s_e-s_i)$; the noncontrastive/raw Jacobian
lens (NEL) decodes $\operatorname{mean}_i J_i s_e$. Logit lens decodes
$s_e$, and differential logit lens decodes
$s_e-\operatorname{mean}_i s_i$. Every readout applies the same final
normalization and output head. Scores are the negative median of
$\log_{10}(\mathrm{rank}+1)$ for zero-based ranks, with the gender rule
above. Every eligible relation uses every original row.

\paragraph{Attribute rate.}
Following \citet{geva-etal-2023-dissecting}, we compare filtered top-50
logit-lens predictions with attributes retrieved from Wikipedia.
The retrieval queries cover every original subject, including
those used as demonstrations or references. The source length,
stopword and retrieval filters are retained. If no prediction survives
the filter, the original overlap fraction is undefined; for this
full-row comparison we explicitly extend it to zero and retain the row.
The accompanying measurements record every such case. This convention
changes the metric for those cases and avoids a model-specific filtered
cohort.

\subsection{OLMo corpus-frequency baselines}
\label{sec:corpus-freq-baselines}
\input{generated/full_evaluation/corpus}
A document contributes at most one mention per subject/object string.
Matching uses normalized literal names with Unicode word boundaries,
without alias resolution or a proximity constraint. The saved subject,
object and joint counts cover every original fact string. We restore
original duplicate multiplicities and recompute relation aggregates
before correlating them with the current LRE outcomes. Unobserved
conditioning marginals receive the explicit zero convention noted in
the table, and no row is dropped.

These associations remain exploratory: relations can share subjects
and objects, the counts do not resolve aliases, and $p$-values are
unadjusted. They are not evidence that training frequency causes the
observed readout or LRE performance.

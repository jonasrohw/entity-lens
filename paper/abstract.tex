\begin{abstract}
Linear relational embeddings (LREs) approximate a language
model's subject-to-object mapping with an affine
function. Their faithfulness varies enormously across
relations, from near zero for \textsc{person\_father} to
the ceiling for \textsc{country\_capital\_city}. We investigate
the factors that contribute to this variation and
demonstrate that it can be significantly predicted from a
readout that is not aware of the underlying relationship. To
do so, we propose a novel form of the Jacobian lens, the
\emph{entity lens}. Whereas the Jacobian lens is estimated
across all tokens and concepts, the entity lens
$\mbox{EL}_t$
is trained
to predict what the model will output for an entity of
type $t$; e.g., we train ELs for countries and for persons.

After estimating an entity lens, we measure how
highly it ranks the model's answer for a relation without
using any relation-specific information in estimation and
prediction.  Across 28 relations of the LRE dataset on
Olmo-3-7B this relation-agnostic score correlates with LRE
faithfulness at $\rho = \headlinenumber$  ($p < .01$).  This
suggests that linear decodability is largely a property of
what a subject's representation already carries, rather than
of the relation being decoded.
Moving the
unit of analysis from the relation to the individual fact,
we conclude that new relation datasets and analysis methods
are needed to address several problems in existing datasets,
most prominently semantically weak first tokens (e.g., most
predicted universities' first token is ``University'') and
reference competition (e.g., the LLM confuses obscure
actor names with competing non-actor entities that have the
same last name).
\end{abstract}

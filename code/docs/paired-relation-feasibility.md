# Same subjects, different relations: feasibility audit

The comparison is possible, and we have concrete matched examples. Existing
relation datasets do not provide a large matched high-versus-low cohort under
the paper's identifying-first-token rule. This audit therefore prepares an
expanded country dataset and a causal protocol. **No new model inference or
paired causal interventions were run in this audit.** Previously saved results
are exploratory evidence for choosing the next experiment.

## What the existing data supports

Counts below match exact subject strings, remove ambiguous subject labels, and
apply the existing OLMo tokenizer filter separately within each original relation.
Alias resolution could increase overlaps but was not guessed.

| Pair | Raw shared subjects | Shared after token filtering | Earlier layer-17 LRE faithfulness |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Country: capital / currency | 13 | 13 | 80.0% / 25.0% |
| Person: native language / father | 44 | 7 | 83.3% / 4.2% |
| Company: headquarters / CEO | 10 | 7 | 45.8% / 0.0% |
| Superhero: identity / archnemesis | 49 | 26 | 45.8% / 16.7% |
| Person: sport / position | 24 | 24 | 87.5% / 70.8% |
| Person: father / mother | 748 | 53 | 4.2% / 4.2% |

The performance columns use the earlier **unmatched** test sets, often small.
They suggest candidate relations, not a demonstrated gap on shared subjects.
Father/mother has the largest filtered overlap but both relations have low LRE
faithfulness. The original landmark-country/continent datasets share only one
subject, despite both being large.

Two matched observations from the completed float32 experiments:

* **Saudi Arabia, layer 17:** the full model predicts the dataset first token
  correctly for both relations. The language LRE predicts `Arabic`; the capital
  LRE predicts `Q` instead of `Riyadh`. Both use validation-selected beta 3.
  Capital LRE succeeds at layers 5 and 11, then fails at 17 and 23. Language LRE
  fails at 5 and 11, then succeeds at 17 and 23. This is a useful depth-dependent
  contrast in one subject, not a relation-level significance result.
* **Australia, layer 17:** the largest-city LRE predicts `Sydney`, matching the
  model. The currency LRE predicts `Australian`, while the model predicts
  `Dollar`. This is a first-token faithfulness difference; the wording may still
  be compatible with the same currency. It is not a clean semantic failure.

For Saudi Arabia at layer 17, replacing the relation-conditioned subject state
with its neutral state rescues the capital LRE and preserves the language LRE.
Replacing the local subject MLP update by its training mean breaks the language
LRE, while the full model still answers both relations correctly. These findings
make state/readout compatibility and alternative computation paths worth testing;
they do not establish disjoint factual channels. The earlier prompts and
demonstrations differ between relations, so these are not identical-state tests.

## Expanded dataset prepared

The artifact directory is
[`generated/paired_relation_feasibility_20260920`](../generated/paired_relation_feasibility_20260920).
It contains the source snapshots, their SHA256 hashes, all exclusion reasons,
paired examples, two relation JSONs, and one shared split manifest.

The sources are [GeoNames country information](https://download.geonames.org/export/dump/countryInfo.txt)
and the [mledoze country dataset](https://github.com/mledoze/countries), retrieved
20 September 2026. GeoNames supplies labels and identifiers; the second source
checks capital names, currency codes, UN membership, and multiplicity. Labels
are sourced, not generated by a language model. These sources can share errors;
agreement is not a complete factuality audit or a historical snapshot of the
model's training knowledge. Preserve source attribution and original license
terms when distributing the derived dataset (GeoNames attribution; mledoze ODbL).

The preparation:

1. Joins by ISO country code and restricts to UN members in the second source.
2. Requires one listed capital and one listed currency in the second source.
3. Requires capital agreement after accent/punctuation normalization and
   identical currency codes. Unresolved variants and disagreements are excluded.
4. Keeps the GeoNames capital and generic currency-unit labels. `Dollar` and
   `Franc` are units shared by multiple ISO currencies, matching the original
   benchmark's label convention. This is not a currency-code task.
5. Applies the existing identifying-first-token rule to both relations and
   keeps their intersection. **171 source-agreement countries become 109
   paired countries**; 62 fail one or both token filters. No vocabulary restriction
   or changed LRE scoring is introduced to retain them.
6. Uses identical subject partitions for both relations: 8 demonstrations,
   8 Jacobian references, 24 direction-training subjects, 16 validation subjects,
   and **53 confirmation subjects**. All 29 retained countries appearing in the
   earlier country datasets are assigned to development, including aliases.

This is a candidate corpus: model knowledge, high/low LRE status, and answer-wording
robustness have not yet been measured on it. The expanded vocabulary also changes
which countries pass the token filter relative to the original small datasets.
Saudi Arabia, for example, is a saved case study but not a member of the new
filtered cohort.

## What would count as enough data?

Power depends on the actual paired difference and the frequency of discordant
outcomes. An exact two-sided McNemar calculation at alpha .05 gives:

| Independent test subjects | True faithfulness gap | Fraction with different success outcomes | Power |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 53 | 30 percentage points | 50% | 86.4% |
| 53 | 20 percentage points | 40% | 57.4% |
| 53 | 20 percentage points | 60% | 39.7% |
| 100 | 20 percentage points | 40% | 87.8% |
| 150 | 20 percentage points | 60% | 87.5% |

The prepared cohort can test a large gap; it cannot guarantee significance or
precise component localization. Restricting to subjects the model answers
correctly for both relations reduces the sample further. Rephrasing the same
subject or rerunning seeds does not create new independent subjects. If a pilot
indicates a smaller effect, expand to a larger entity domain with sourced
attributes, or report the resulting uncertainty; do not keep adding subjects
until a p-value crosses .05.

## Proposed mechanistic experiment

### 1. Establish the paired performance contrast

Evaluate both relations on precisely the same subjects. Choose layers, gains,
templates, and any channel ranks on development data only. Use the previous
unmatched results to nominate capital/currency; do not choose a new winning
pair from confirmation results and then treat it as preregistered.

Report each relation's LRE agreement, full-model factual correctness, target
log probability, output margin, and offset-only baseline. Include all subjects
and the prespecified subset where both model answers are correct. Match or
stratify on model confidence, subject length/familiarity, answer token frequency,
and answer-class prevalence. Currency's repeated classes need a class-balanced
secondary analysis. Check the gap under controlled answer wording before
attributing it to computation. An LRE first token alone cannot establish that an
alternative full answer would be semantically correct.

### 2. Hold the subject state exactly fixed

Use prompts with an identical prefix through the subject's last token and put
the relation after it, for example:

```
France has the capital city of
France uses the currency called
```

Use either no demonstrations or the same combined demonstration prefix in both
conditions. Different relation-specific demonstrations would break the control.
Verify token IDs, positions, and hidden states through the subject token are
identical. Causal attention then makes the subject state identical before the
relation suffix is read. The suffix changes which later computation reads it.
Replicate with original prompt styles separately to check external validity;
the high/low gap must be re-established under this controlled prompt design.

### 3. Test whether relations use different representation directions

Define a channel as a subspace of the subject residual state, not necessarily a
single coordinate, neuron, or attention head. Use exact full-model gradients
of answer-versus-foil logit margins over training subjects to propose directions
for A and B. Choose matched ranks using validation. Deriving both channels only
from LRE matrices would be unreliable for the relation the LRE already fails to
approximate. Compare subspace overlap, but treat this as descriptive.

The causal test applies each learned projection intervention under **both**
relations. Replace its subject-specific projection with a training-mean or
matched-donor projection. Measure the full model and LRE separately:

| Intervention | Relation A effect | Relation B effect |
|---|---|---|
| Remove A-associated subspace | Delta(A,A) | Delta(A,B) |
| Remove B-associated subspace | Delta(B,A) | Delta(B,B) |

Evidence for distinct channels would be a replicated double dissociation:
removing A directions hurts A more than B, and removing B directions hurts B
more than A. Compare equal-rank, equal-perturbation-norm random controls, several
donors/strengths, and preservation of unrelated subject information. Use a paired
relation-by-intervention interaction on continuous margins/log probabilities,
not just separate significance tests. Subspace orthogonality alone is insufficient.

### 4. Test whether low LRE reflects nonlinear or context-dependent decoding

In a fixed recipient prompt, patch the subject state along a donor direction:

```
s(t) = s_recipient + t * (s_donor - s_recipient)
t = 0, .25, .5, .75, 1
```

Compare the actual final hidden vector with its exact local first-order Taylor
prediction, and with the global reference-averaged LRE. Set beta=1 for the
mathematical Taylor test; evaluate validation-scaled LRE predictions separately.
Use donors matched in state distance and known-answer status across relations.
For a sufficiently small step, verify finite-difference agreement numerically.

Larger Taylor residuals or faster Jacobian changes for the low-LRE relation would
support greater nonlinearity along tested directions. If local Taylor predictions
work but the global LRE fails, the issue may be variation between subjects or
reference contexts. If hidden-vector errors are comparable but token agreement
differs, output geometry, confidence, or wording is a better candidate explanation.
Off-manifold interpolation and unchanged information at other token positions
limit what this assay establishes.

### 5. Locate differences in actual model computation

Use the same corrupted donor subject under both relations and exact activation
patching of clean recipient components. Scan attention/MLP blocks and meaningful
token positions, then inspect heads only in blocks nominated on development
subjects. The final subject token, other subject subtokens, relation suffix, and
answer position can carry different paths. Do not restrict the whole scan to the
subject-token components used in the previous experiment.

For each held-out subject compare causal effects under both relations. A
relation-selective patching pattern, combined with subspace-selective effects,
would support different routes. To connect a route specifically to LRE failure,
test whether interventions on that route alter the full-model-minus-LRE residual;
a heatmap difference alone does not explain a faithfulness gap. State restoration
and identity patches are implementation checks, not independent causal findings.

All future model work should preserve float32/eager attention and run on rho1 or
rho2 when local GPU space is insufficient, as requested. No approximate attribution
patching, quantization, or numerical shortcut is required by this design.

## Reproduce the completed audit

From `code/`, with the two source files in the saved `sources/` directory:

```
HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1 .venv/bin/python -m jlens.paired_relation_audit
```

The audit reads the exact checkpoint tokenizer, existing result files and local
datasets, computes all overlap counts, extracts matched case studies, prepares
the shared split, and calculates exact illustrative paired-test power. It asserts
unique subject/ISO identities, disjoint partitions, complete assignment, and
exclusion of previously used country subjects from confirmation.
